# Superstore Sales Analysis

I picked up the classic [Superstore dataset from Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/vivek468/superstore-dataset-final) and wanted to do something more than the usual `.head()` → `.describe()` → bar chart routine. The goal was to treat it like an actual business problem: where is the store making money, where is it bleeding profit, and what should change?

## What's inside

The notebook covers ~10,000 transactions across 4 years (2014–2017) of a US retail store. Here's what I looked at:

**The basics (cleaned up)**
- Data loading, null/duplicate checks, date parsing, feature engineering
- Sales and profit breakdown by category, sub-category, region, and city

**The interesting stuff**
- **Discount vs. profitability** — turns out discounts above 20% make the average order unprofitable. Tables and Bookcases lose money at almost *any* discount level. This was the biggest finding for me.
- **Segment × Region profit matrix** — heatmaps showing exactly which customer-segment + region combos make money and which don't. The Central region has a real problem.
- **Shipping mode analysis** — does Same Day shipping hurt or help margins? (spoiler: it's fine, maybe even better)
- **RFM customer segmentation** — scored all 793 customers on Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value. Identified Champions, at-risk accounts, and lost customers.
- **Sales trends & seasonality** — monthly time series with a trendline, year-over-year growth rates, and month-over-month volatility. Q4 is king.

Every section ends with a "So What?" — a few bullets translating the numbers into something a business person would actually act on.

## Key findings (the short version)

1. **Discounting is the #1 profit killer.** Cap Furniture discounts at 20%. Seriously.
2. **Technology carries the business** — 37% of sales but 51% of profit. Invest here.
3. **Central region** generates good revenue but terrible margins, mostly from over-discounting Furniture to Consumer customers.
4. **Home Office customers** are the most profitable segment per order despite being the smallest. They deserve more attention.
5. **~20% YoY growth** with heavy Q4 seasonality. Plan inventory and staffing accordingly.

## How to run

1. Clone this repo
2. Make sure you have the dependencies: `pip install pandas matplotlib seaborn numpy chardet`
3. Open the notebook in Jupyter / VS Code / Kaggle and run all cells

The CSV is expected to be in the same folder as the notebook.
